# Dungeon Tile Types and Procedures

Below is a list of the **12 distinct tile (chamber) types** in the game, each governed by its own specific rule or procedure when entered:

1. **Dungeon Room**: Draw a Dungeon card.

1. **Bottomless Pit**: Test Luck. If you succeed, you cross safely; if you fail, your Hero falls and is killed.

1. **Catacomb Entrance**: Draw a Dungeon card. On your next turn, you have the option to enter the Catacombs instead of moving normally.

1. **Trap Room**: Draw a Trap card.

1. **Tower Room**: Located at the corners of the board. If you possess one or more Loot cards, you may choose to exit and win/survive the dungeon; otherwise, you must immediately move out of it.

1. **Chamber of Darkness**: You must immediately move again. Roll a six-sided die to randomly determine which passage you must exit through; if that passage is blocked by a wall, your turn ends there, and you must roll again next turn.

1. **Treasure Chamber**: Located at the center of the board. You must draw a Dragon card to see if Dragonlord Kalladra awakens.

1. **Corridor**: Forces you to immediately move again, with the restriction that you cannot enter the exact same Corridor tile twice during a single turn.

1. **Chasm**: Draw a Dungeon card. Because an uncrossable fissure splits the tile, on your next turn you can only exit through a passage located on the same side of the fissure you entered from.

1. **Cave-in**: Draw a Dungeon card. On your next turn, you must choose to either retreat to the chamber you came from or test your Agility to successfully cross the debris. Failing the test leaves you trapped there for the turn.

1. **Bridge**: You must decide to either stay on your entry side (requiring you to return to your previous chamber on your next turn) or attempt to cross the bridge by testing Agility with a +1 penalty/modifier added to your dice roll.

1. **Rotating Room**: You must immediately rotate the tile 180 degrees upon entry, which can potentially block your path and result in a dead end.
